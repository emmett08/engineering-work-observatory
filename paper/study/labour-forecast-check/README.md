# Checking the intake forecast against known labour

From the repository root run `python3 paper/study/labour-forecast-check/check.py`. The script implements the historical mean in the [paper’s forecasting section](../../article/sampling-validation.tex). Each row of its output gives the eligible history size for that task class, both predictions and the known hours. An empty prediction means that the available history was too short. The repository’s separate portfolio forecast estimates pull-request merge counts.

The fixture follows 13 distinct requirements for one synthetic team. The first five provide an initial history. The next eight constitute the evaluation population. Each labour window closes two days after intake. At each later intake the script uses all earlier hours disclosed by then, including those for previously evaluated requirements once their own windows have closed. One abandoned fix took zero hours and its measurement arrived two days late. Each forecast requires two eligible observations. The class-specific method uses earlier requirements of the same type with no fallback. The comparator pools all types.

The class-specific method issued seven forecasts. The new migration class had no earlier observations. The pooled method issued eight. On the seven tasks for which both methods issued a forecast, the respective mean absolute errors were 1.19 and 2.48 person-hours. The two fixes from day 10 onwards each took six hours. The class mean predicted two hours for the first and eight-thirds of an hour for the next. Both subsequent incidents took the predicted nine hours. Individual errors reveal the change in the fix tasks that the overall mean can conceal.

These results check intake-time eligibility, forecast availability and scoring against constructed labour totals. Their numerical values follow from the chosen histories. A workplace evaluation requires forecasts issued before work begins and independently assessed hours for consecutive eligible intakes, including the tasks on which a method declines to issue a forecast.
