\subsection{Frozen predictions as auxiliary information}
\label{sec:model-assisted}

The difference estimator starts with a fixed prediction of total labour and corrects it using a probability sample of allocation observations. Inverse inclusion probabilities make the weighted residual sum unbiased for total prediction error under the conditions below. Denis and Haddou give the fixed-prediction form and its design variance explicitly \cite[Section~2.3, equations~(2)--(4)]{denishaddou2026}. Prediction-powered inference also identifies its mean construction as a difference estimator and considers finite-population extensions \cite[Section~1.4 and Appendix~B of the accompanying preprint]{angelopoulos2023}.

Partition each person's target observation period into non-overlapping intervals. Let $U=\{1,\ldots,M\}$, $M\geq1$, be the complete, fixed frame of these person-intervals. For interval $m$, let $d_m>0$ be its length in hours and $a_m\in[0,1]$ its average allocation to the target work. Its labour is $y_m=d_ma_m$ person-hours, and the population total is $H=\sum_Uy_m$. For every frame unit, retain a prediction $f_m$ in person-hours. A raw SCH value requires a stated conversion to labour units before it can serve as $f_m$. The entire prediction vector must be fixed under the sampling design. Training on an independent earlier population can satisfy this condition: condition on the frozen predictions, then sample allocations to correct their total error.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-prediction difference estimator]
\label{prop:difference-estimator}
For a probability sample $s\subseteq U$ with known first-order inclusion probabilities $\pi_m>0$, set $r_m=y_m-f_m$ and
\begin{equation}
 \widehat H_{\mathrm{diff}}
 =\sum_{m\in U}f_m+\sum_{m\in s}\frac{y_m-f_m}{\pi_m}.
 \label{eq:difference-estimator}
\end{equation}
If $y_m$ and $f_m$ are fixed under the design and every sampled labour value is observed accurately, then $\E_s[\widehat H_{\mathrm{diff}}]=H$, where $\E_s$ averages over the sampling design. Neither independence between inclusion indicators nor correct predictions are required.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $I_m=\ind\{m\in s\}$. Linearity and $\E_s[I_m]=\pi_m$ give
\[
 \E_s[\widehat H_{\mathrm{diff}}]
 =\sum_Uf_m+\sum_U\frac{\E_s[I_m]}{\pi_m}(y_m-f_m)
 =\sum_Uy_m.
\]
\end{proof}

The residual correction makes the estimate design-unbiased even when the fixed prediction total differs from $H$. Taking $f_m=0$ gives the Horvitz--Thompson (HT) estimator \cite{horvitz1952},
\begin{equation}
 \widehat H_{\mathrm{HT}}=\sum_{m\in s}\frac{d_ma_m}{\pi_m}.
 \label{eq:ht}
\end{equation}
Record sampled zero allocations as observations. An unanswered sampling prompt is nonresponse and must remain distinguishable from zero labour and from an unselected unit. The proof concerns true allocation values. Unbiasedness in the presence of measurement error requires additional assumptions on that error.

\subsection{Precision depends on residuals and the sampling design}
\label{sec:difference-variance}

Let $\pi_{mk}=\E_s[I_mI_k]$, including $\pi_{mm}=\pi_m$. The exact design variance is
\begin{equation}
 \Var_s(\widehat H_{\mathrm{diff}})
 =\sum_{m,k\in U}
 \frac{\pi_{mk}-\pi_m\pi_k}{\pi_m\pi_k}\,r_mr_k.
 \label{eq:difference-general-variance}
\end{equation}
Expanding the variance of $\sum_U I_mr_m/\pi_m$ gives this expression. Unlike the unbiasedness proof, the variance calculation uses covariances between inclusion indicators, which require joint inclusion probabilities. The population identity in \eqref{eq:difference-general-variance} also involves generally unknown unsampled residuals. Estimating it requires the actual sampling design and the joint-inclusion conditions of the chosen variance estimator.

For simple random sampling without replacement from $M\geq2$ units, let the fixed sample size satisfy $1\leq n\leq M$. Every size-$n$ subset has the same probability and $\pi_m=n/M$. Write
\[
 S_r^2=\frac{1}{M-1}\sum_U(r_m-\bar r_U)^2,
 \qquad
 S_y^2=\frac{1}{M-1}\sum_U(y_m-\bar y_U)^2.
\]
The inclusion covariances in \eqref{eq:difference-general-variance} give
\begin{equation}
 \Var_s(\widehat H_{\mathrm{diff}})
 =\frac{M^2}{n}\left(1-\frac nM\right)S_r^2,
 \qquad
 \Var_s(\widehat H_{\mathrm{HT}})
 =\frac{M^2}{n}\left(1-\frac nM\right)S_y^2.
 \label{eq:difference-srs-variance}
\end{equation}
For $2\leq n\leq M$, an unbiased estimator of the design variance is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat V_{\mathrm{diff}}
 =\frac{M^2}{n}\left(1-\frac nM\right)s_r^2,
 \qquad
 s_r^2=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{m\in s}(r_m-\bar r_s)^2.
 \label{eq:difference-srs-variance-estimator}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $s_r^2=[n(n-1)]^{-1}\sum_{m<k,\,m,k\in s}(r_m-r_k)^2$. Each pair has inclusion probability $n(n-1)/[M(M-1)]$, so $\E_s[s_r^2]=S_r^2$. A noncensus sample of size one does not supply this variance estimate. A complete census has zero sampling variance, including a single-unit population. Measurement error remains a separate question.

For $n<M$, the difference estimator improves on HT exactly when $S_r^2<S_y^2$. The gain in sampling precision therefore depends on residual variation under the design. Consider $M=4$, $n=2$, and $y=(0,2,4,6)$. The true total is $12$, and HT variance is $80/3$. Frozen predictions $f=(1,3,3,5)$ yield variance $16/3$, whereas $f=(6,4,2,0)$ yield $320/3$. Enumerating all six samples reproduces these variances and unbiased totals. It also verifies that \eqref{eq:difference-srs-variance-estimator} averages to each corresponding variance. The second predictor increases variance fourfold. The difference estimator is design-unbiased with either predictor, while its precision differs. The finite checks establish the calculations for these two predictors. A workplace study must determine whether code-derived predictions reduce residual variation under its sampling design.

\subsection{Failure cases and implementation checks}
\label{sec:difference-contract}

The fixed-vector proof does not apply when predictions are fitted to the realised sample. For $M=2$, $n=1$ and $y=(0,2)$, suppose a fitted rule memorises the selected outcome and predicts zero for the other unit. Its observed residual is always zero. The difference total is nevertheless $0$ or $2$, each with probability $1/2$, giving expectation $1$ rather than $H=2$. A sample-trained method needs a separate argument under the actual finite-population design.

The estimate can also be negative even though true labour is nonnegative. With $y=(0,0)$, $f=(10,0)$ and one uniformly selected unit, \eqref{eq:difference-estimator} is $-10$ or $10$ with equal probability. Clipping at zero changes its expectation from $0$ to $5$. Preserve the signed estimate when reporting this estimator and investigate an implausible result. Dividing retained output by an unbiased labour estimate also need not yield an unbiased productivity estimate.

The accompanying implementation preserves signed estimates and observed zeros, and records nonresponse separately from unselected units. It rejects incomplete declarations, nonresponse and inconsistent inputs. For a general declared design it reports point estimates. For simple random sampling it also reports variance and standard error under the conditions above. These checks assess consistency of the supplied records. Establishing frame completeness, allocation accuracy and compliance with the declared design requires evidence from collection.

Exhaustive finite tests check the examples above, input validation and census boundaries. The implementation supplies no default confidence interval: unbiased variance estimation establishes neither a normal approximation nor finite-sample interval coverage.
