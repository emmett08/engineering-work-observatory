\section{Attainable labour ranges from interval observations}
\label{sec:sharp-identification}

Knowing how many person-hours occurred during an interval need not tell us when within that interval people worked. We seek every labour total that could have occurred within the specified target windows while satisfying the observations and the allocation constraint in \eqref{eq:labour}. These totals form the \emph{identified range}: for each total, at least one way of allocating people's time satisfies all the observations and constraints. No allocation satisfying them gives a total outside this range. This definition follows the general partial-identification perspective \cite[Section~1]{torgovitsky2019}. The following proofs derive these totals from the stated observation bounds and allocation constraints.

\subsection{Using the person's capacity}

One person can contribute at most one person-hour in an elapsed hour. Knowing who supplied an interval total therefore limits how much of that labour could have occurred before or after a specified time. A total across several people must be compared with their combined available time.

\begin{proposition}[Attainable contribution from one interval]
\label{prop:sharp-cutoff}
Let $\ell<u$, $c\in[\ell,u]$ and $v\in[0,u-\ell]$. Among measurable allocations $a:[\ell,u]\to[0,1]$ with $\int_\ell^u a(t)\,dt=v$, the attainable values of $x=\int_\ell^c a(t)\,dt$ are exactly
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sharp-cutoff}
 \left[\max\{0,v-(u-c)\},\;\min\{v,c-\ell\}\right].
\end{equation}
If $v<0$ or $v>u-\ell$, no such allocation exists.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Nonnegativity and capacity on the two pieces imply
\[
 0\leq x\leq c-\ell,\qquad 0\leq v-x\leq u-c.
\]
Together, these inequalities give \eqref{eq:sharp-cutoff}. Conversely, for any $x$ in this interval, set $a=x/(c-\ell)$ before $c$ and $a=(v-x)/(u-c)$ after $c$. Each value lies in $[0,1]$ and gives the required integral. Omit a zero-length piece when $c$ is an endpoint: the corresponding numerator is then zero. Finally, integrating $0\leq a\leq1$ gives the necessary condition $0\leq v\leq u-\ell$.
\end{proof}

For one person, $3/2$ person-hours in a two-hour interval imply between $1/2$ and $1$ person-hours in its first hour. Nonnegativity alone would give $[0,3/2]$. In this example, the bounds do not determine the first-hour total or exactly when the person worked. If $v=u-\ell$, the total uses all the person's available time, so the labour before any time within it equals the elapsed time from its start. For an interior subinterval $[b,c]\subseteq[\ell,u]$, applying the same construction to that subinterval and the time outside it gives every possible labour total in the subinterval: $[\max\{0,v-((u-\ell)-(c-b))\},\min\{v,c-b\}]$.

Bounds calculated separately for each contribution can be added when each contribution is counted once. The summed lower bound is possible only if allocations achieving every component minimum can occur together; the same condition applies to the upper bound and component maxima. Disjoint intervals of one person, or intervals belonging to distinct people, can be combined when no other constraints link them. Overlapping observations or several tasks competing for the same person's time require a joint calculation. For example, one person spends one person-hour on each of two tasks during $[0,2]$. Each task separately allows $[0,1]$ person-hours before time 1. Together, the tasks require exactly one person-hour before time 1, because they use all the person's available time on $[0,2]$. Adding the separately calculated bounds would give $[0,2]$. Figure~\ref{fig:shared-capacity} plots the separately permitted and jointly feasible first-hour totals.

\subsection{Replacing allocation histories with interval totals}

To account for tasks sharing a person's time, call each task--person--role combination a stream. Fix finite sets of tasks $\mathcal I$, people $\mathcal J$ and roles $\mathcal R$, a cohort $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal I$, and a bounded calendar-time domain $[b_0,b_K]$ with $b_0<b_K$ containing every observation interval and target window. An admissible history consists of measurable $a_{ijr}\geq0$ satisfying $\sum_{i,r}a_{ijr}(t)\leq1$ almost everywhere for every person $j$. The declared roster and rules for assigning time to tasks determine whose labour and which work are included. Whether these declarations cover the intended work requires measurement evidence. Work outside $\mathcal C$ can be recorded separately when it uses the same people's time without contributing to cohort labour.

For observations $\nu=1,\ldots,M$, specify an interval $[\ell_\nu,u_\nu]$ within this domain, a set $G_\nu\subseteq\mathcal I\times\mathcal J\times\mathcal R$ of attributed streams, and finite labour bounds $v_\nu^-\leq v_\nu^+$, in person-hours. Impose
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:interval-information}
 v_\nu^-\leq
 \sum_{(i,j,r)\in G_\nu}\int_{\ell_\nu}^{u_\nu}a_{ijr}(t)\,dt
 \leq v_\nu^+.
\end{equation}
Exact interval totals use $v_\nu^-=v_\nu^+$. Justified limits on rounding or measurement error may instead give different lower and upper bounds. A Horvitz--Thompson estimate varies with the selected sample; that sampling uncertainty requires a separate analysis before the estimate can justify either inequality.

Form the sorted distinct endpoints $b_0<\cdots<b_K$ from the domain boundaries, every observation endpoint and every target boundary. Call the intervals between successive endpoints cells. For the cohort denominator, target boundaries include each cohort task's enrolment $s_i$ and the fixed end of its labour observation window $c_i=s_i+\tau+\Delta$. Translate task ages into calendar time before partitioning, so that simultaneous work by the same person shares one capacity constraint. Keep cells outside a target window whenever an observation spans them: some of the observed labour may have occurred outside the window. Put $d_k=b_k-b_{k-1}>0$ and let $x_{ijrk}$ denote the labour allocated to stream $(i,j,r)$ in cell $k$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact cell reduction and attainable labour range]
\label{prop:cell-reduction}
Under the preceding model, define the finite-dimensional feasible set $\mathcal P$ by
\begin{align}
\label{eq:cell-capacity}
 x_{ijrk}&\geq0,&\sum_{i,r}x_{ijrk}&\leq d_k
 &&\text{for every }j,k,\\
\label{eq:cell-information}
 v_\nu^-&\leq
 \sum_{(i,j,r)\in G_\nu}
 \sum_{k:\,[b_{k-1},b_k]\subseteq[\ell_\nu,u_\nu]}x_{ijrk}
 \leq v_\nu^+ &&\text{for every }\nu.
\end{align}
Fix real weights $w_{ijrk}$ and define the target for a continuous-time allocation history by multiplying each cell integral by $w_{ijrk}$ and summing. For its finite representation $h(x)=\sum_{i,j,r,k}w_{ijrk}x_{ijrk}$, the attainable target values over admissible histories equal $h(\mathcal P)$. In particular, the fixed-horizon cohort denominator uses $w_{ijrk}=1$ exactly when $i\in\mathcal C$ and $[b_{k-1},b_k]\subseteq[s_i,c_i]$, and zero otherwise. If $\mathcal P$ is nonempty, its target range is the closed interval
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sharp-labour-range}
 [H_{\min},H_{\max}]
 =\left[\min_{x\in\mathcal P}h(x),\;\max_{x\in\mathcal P}h(x)\right].
\end{equation}
Both endpoints and every value between them are attained. If $\mathcal P$ is empty, the observations and capacity assumptions are inconsistent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Integrating any admissible history over each cell gives nonnegative cell labour totals. Integrating each person's allocation limit gives \eqref{eq:cell-capacity}. Splitting each observation at the cell boundaries gives \eqref{eq:cell-information}. The weighted target total is unchanged because its weights are constant on cells.

Conversely, for $x\in\mathcal P$ set $a_{ijr}(t)=x_{ijrk}/d_k$ in the interior of cell $k$, and set the finitely many endpoint values to zero. These measurable allocations are nonnegative and have sum at most one for each person. They reproduce every cell labour total, so all observation bounds remain satisfied and the target value is unchanged. Thus the original allocation functions and the finite feasible set give exactly the same possible target values.

The constraints define a closed convex set. Each coordinate is between zero and $d_k$, so a nonempty $\mathcal P$ is compact and a linear functional attains its extrema. Convex combinations of minimising and maximising points attain every intermediate value. The reconstruction just given supplies an admissible history for each of them.
\end{proof}

The minimum and maximum target totals can be computed by linear programmes, each with $|\mathcal I||\mathcal J||\mathcal R|K$ variables, $|\mathcal J|K$ shared-capacity constraints and $2M$ observation inequalities, in addition to nonnegativity. For every assignment of cell labour totals satisfying the linear constraints, the proof constructs time allocations that satisfy the original observations and give the same target total. A known period of unavailability can be included by adding its boundaries to the partition and setting that person's cell labour totals to zero within it. This exact correspondence depends on allowing measurable fractional allocations. Minimum session lengths or restrictions on task interruption would limit which time allocations are permitted. Smoothness constraints and switching costs would also require a separate analysis.

For the cohort-denominator target, let $Q=\sum_{i\in\mathcal C}Q_i(\tau,\Delta)$ be retained output known exactly. With $Q\geq0$ and $H_{\min}>0$, dividing this output by the largest and smallest possible labour totals gives the identified productivity interval $[Q/H_{\max},Q/H_{\min}]$. Justified quality bounds $0\leq Q_-\leq Q\leq Q_+$ give an interval $[Q_-/H_{\max},Q_+/H_{\min}]$ containing every feasible output-to-labour ratio. It is the exact range only when every ratio in it can be produced by an output total and labour total that are jointly permitted. It is sufficient for every pair in $[Q_-,Q_+]\times[H_{\min},H_{\max}]$ to be permitted. A lower labour bound of zero cannot be used as a divisor. The acceptance clock and retention assessment in \eqref{eq:retention} remain fixed throughout the allocation optimisation.
