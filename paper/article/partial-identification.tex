\section{What interval observations determine about labour}
\label{sec:sharp-identification}

A two-day total can leave the first day's hours uncertain. The \emph{identified range} contains precisely the totals allowed by the observations and each person's time limit in \eqref{eq:labour} \cite[Section~1]{torgovitsky2019}. When the range contains just one value, the hours are known exactly.

\subsection{One person's available time}

If a two-day total exceeds the hours one person could work on the second day, that person must have worked at least the excess on the first.

Let $v$ be the person's labour between $\ell$ and $u$. If $x$ hours fall before the cutoff $c$, the remaining $v-x$ must fit between $c$ and $u$.\footnote{The allocation $a(t)$ gives the fraction of the person's time devoted to the work at instant $t$. In Proposition~\ref{prop:nonidentification}, $c$ denotes a probability.}

\begin{proposition}[Hours worked before a cutoff given the interval total]
\label{prop:sharp-cutoff}
Let $\ell<u$, $c\in[\ell,u]$ and $v\in[0,u-\ell]$. As $a$ ranges over measurable allocations $a:[\ell,u]\to[0,1]$ with $\int_\ell^u a(t)\,dt=v$, the values of $x=\int_\ell^c a(t)\,dt$ fill the interval
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sharp-cutoff}
 \left[\max\{0,v-(u-c)\},\;\min\{v,c-\ell\}\right].
\end{equation}
If $v<0$ or $v>u-\ell$, no such allocation exists.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Capacity on either side of $c$ requires
\[
 0\leq x\leq c-\ell,\qquad 0\leq v-x\leq u-c.
\]
These inequalities give \eqref{eq:sharp-cutoff}. For any $x$ in that range and $\ell<c<u$, use the allocation
\[
 a(t)=\begin{cases}
 x/(c-\ell),&\ell\leq t<c,\\
 (v-x)/(u-c),&c\leq t\leq u.
 \end{cases}
\]
Both fractions lie in $[0,1]$. Integrating gives
\begin{align*}
 \int_\ell^c a(t)\,dt&=\frac{x}{c-\ell}(c-\ell)=x,\\
 \int_c^u a(t)\,dt&=\frac{v-x}{u-c}(u-c)=v-x.
\end{align*}
The two integrals sum to $v$. For $c=\ell$ and $c=u$, the constant allocation $a(t)=v/(u-\ell)$ gives $x=0$ and $x=v$, respectively. Integrating $0\leq a\leq1$ over the whole interval excludes totals outside $[0,u-\ell]$.
\end{proof}

Someone who worked $3/2$ hours in a two-hour window could fit at most one hour into the second hour. First-hour labour therefore ranges from $1/2$ to one hour, compared with $[0,3/2]$ under nonnegativity alone.

If $v=u-\ell$, the person works throughout except on a set of duration zero, giving $x=c-\ell$. For any subinterval $[b,c]\subseteq[\ell,u]$, the same argument divides $v$ between that subinterval and its complement. The hours within $[b,c]$ range over $[\max\{0,v-((u-\ell)-(c-b))\},\min\{v,c-b\}]$.

For two tasks sharing one person's time, count each hour once. Adding the task maxima bounds total labour from above, and adding the minima bounds it from below. Either sum is exact if and only if one feasible schedule gives both component values. Schedules for different people or disjoint periods can be combined if the other constraints permit.

In Figure~\ref{fig:shared-capacity}, one person worked an hour on each of two tasks during $[0,2]$. Either task alone could occupy zero to one hour before time 1. Adding their separate ranges gives $[0,2]$, yet the two tasks exhaust the available time. Exactly one hour of labour must fall before time 1. Panel (c) splits it equally between the tasks.

\subsection{Replacing allocation histories with interval totals}

Call the work of person $j$ on task $i$ in activity $r$ a \emph{stream}. Write $\mathcal I$, $\mathcal J$ and $\mathcal R$ for the finite sets of tasks, contributors and activities. The cohort comprises the tasks in $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal I$. Choose a bounded calendar interval $[b_0,b_K]$, with $b_0<b_K$, containing every observation interval and target window. An admissible history specifies a measurable allocation $a_{ijr}\geq0$ for each stream, with $\sum_{i,r}a_{ijr}(t)\leq1$ almost everywhere for each person $j$.

Identify everyone who contributed to the cohort and specify which activities count towards each task. Include work outside $\mathcal C$ when observations of it limit the hours available to the cohort, giving those outside hours zero target weight.

For observation $\nu=1,\ldots,M$, let $G_\nu\subseteq\mathcal I\times\mathcal J\times\mathcal R$ contain the streams covered during $[\ell_\nu,u_\nu]$. Their combined person-hours satisfy the finite observation limits $v_\nu^-\leq v_\nu^+$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:interval-information}
 v_\nu^-\leq
 \sum_{(i,j,r)\in G_\nu}\int_{\ell_\nu}^{u_\nu}a_{ijr}(t)\,dt
 \leq v_\nu^+.
\end{equation}
An exact total has $v_\nu^-=v_\nu^+$. For rounded or imperfect measurements, set limits wide enough to cover the error. Bounds based on a Horvitz--Thompson estimate must also allow for variation across samples.

Use one calendar for all contributors, including when tasks have different intake dates.

Sort the distinct domain, observation and target boundaries as $b_0<\cdots<b_K$. The intervals between successive boundaries are \emph{cells}. Each observation and target window spans whole cells. For the denominator in \eqref{eq:cohort-productivity}, include each cohort task's intake $s_i$ and labour cutoff $c_i=s_i+\tau+\Delta$.

Retain cells beyond the target window when an observation extends into them. Cell $k$ lasts $d_k=b_k-b_{k-1}>0$ hours. Integrating $a_{ijr}(t)$ over it gives the stream's person-hours $x_{ijrk}$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact labour range from cell totals]
\label{prop:cell-reduction}
Under the preceding model, define the finite-dimensional feasible set $\mathcal P$ by
\begin{align}
\label{eq:cell-capacity}
 x_{ijrk}&\geq0,&\sum_{i,r}x_{ijrk}&\leq d_k
 &&\text{for every }j,k,\\
\label{eq:cell-information}
 v_\nu^-&\leq
 \sum_{(i,j,r)\in G_\nu}
 \sum_{k:\,[b_{k-1},b_k]\subseteq[\ell_\nu,u_\nu]}x_{ijrk}
 \leq v_\nu^+ &&\text{for every }\nu.
\end{align}
A history with cell integrals $x_{ijrk}$ has target $h(x)=\sum_{i,j,r,k}w_{ijrk}x_{ijrk}$ for fixed real weights $w_{ijrk}$. The target values over all histories satisfying the observation and capacity constraints form the set $h(\mathcal P)$. The denominator in \eqref{eq:cohort-productivity} uses $w_{ijrk}=1$ exactly when $i\in\mathcal C$ and $[b_{k-1},b_k]\subseteq[s_i,c_i]$, and zero otherwise. If $\mathcal P$ is nonempty, its target range is the closed interval
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sharp-labour-range}
 [H_{\min},H_{\max}]
 =\left[\min_{x\in\mathcal P}h(x),\;\max_{x\in\mathcal P}h(x)\right].
\end{equation}
Every value in this interval, including each endpoint, is the target of a history in which $a_{ijr}(t)\in\{0,1\}$ and each person performs at most one activity on one task at any instant. If $\mathcal P$ is empty, no history satisfies the observations and capacity limits.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Integrating nonnegative allocations and each person's time limit over a cell gives \eqref{eq:cell-capacity}. Summing the hours over an observation's cells and streams gives \eqref{eq:cell-information}. The weights are constant within cells, so replacing the integrals by cell totals preserves the target.

Conversely, take any $x\in\mathcal P$. Within each cell, arrange each person's streams in any order and give each a consecutive block of length $x_{ijrk}$, skipping zero lengths. The capacity inequality leaves room for all blocks and any remaining time is idle. Use half-open blocks and include the final domain endpoint in the last block. During each working block the person devotes all their time to its stream. This schedule is measurable, switches finitely often and reproduces every cell total, hence all observations and the target $h(x)$.

The finite-dimensional set $\mathcal P$ is closed and convex, with each coordinate in $[0,d_k]$. If nonempty, it is compact, so the continuous function $h$ has a minimiser and a maximiser in $\mathcal P$. The segment between them stays in $\mathcal P$ by convexity. Linearity gives every intermediate value on that segment. The block schedule for each of its points uses only allocations in $\{0,1\}$.
\end{proof}

The two endpoint problems share $|\mathcal I||\mathcal J||\mathcal R|K$ nonnegative variables, $|\mathcal J|K$ person--cell capacity constraints and $2M$ observation inequalities. To include a known absence, split the cells at its start and end, then set the person's labour to zero throughout the absence.

Each person needs at most $|\mathcal I||\mathcal R|+1$ constant blocks per cell, including idle time. Switching can occur at any instant. Minimum session lengths, dependencies and limits on interruption may exclude schedules. Smoothness requirements or switching costs need additional constraints or analysis.

A feasible schedule places the observed hours in the calendar. Where several sequences fit the observations, distinguishing the actual order requires further evidence. Acceptance times and delivered output require observations of those outcomes. Extra workplace restrictions can only narrow \eqref{eq:sharp-labour-range}. An endpoint disappears if every schedule giving that total violates a restriction.

On a finite partition, the schedule illustrates the Dvoretzky--Wald--Wolfowitz purification theorem \cite[Theorem~4]{dvoretzky1951ranges}.\footnote{For a finite set of activities and a finite collection of atomless measures, purification preserves the specified integrals while selecting one activity at each instant. Time on a cell is atomless because any measurable set of positive duration can be divided into measurable subsets of smaller positive duration. Idle time accounts for unused capacity.} For the general result see \cite[Section~2]{khanrathsun2006}.

\subsection{Verifying endpoints and allowing for observation error}

A feasible schedule certifies a proposed maximum when its total equals an upper bound valid for all feasible schedules. For a minimum, its total must equal a valid lower bound.

Write $x$ for the cell totals and $w$ for their weights. Express the constraints as $Ax\leq b$ with $x\geq0$, reversing the sign of each lower observation inequality.\footnote{Each row of $A$ gives the coefficients of one constraint, and the corresponding entry of $b$ gives its upper limit. Vector inequalities hold coordinate by coordinate. The superscript $\mathsf T$ denotes transpose, so $w^\mathsf{T}x$ is the target in person-hours when the weights are dimensionless.} Nonnegative multipliers $\lambda$ and $\mu$ give the following bounds.
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bound-certificates}
 A^\mathsf{T}\lambda\geq w,\qquad
 A^\mathsf{T}\mu\geq-w
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 -\mu^\mathsf{T}b\leq h(x)\leq\lambda^\mathsf{T}b
 \qquad(x\in\mathcal P)
\end{equation}
Multiplying the dual and primal inequalities by $x\geq0$ and $\lambda\geq0$, respectively, gives $w^\mathsf{T}x\leq\lambda^\mathsf{T}Ax\leq\lambda^\mathsf{T}b$. Applying the same reasoning to $-w$ gives the lower bound.

For nonempty $\mathcal P$, strong duality guarantees multipliers whose bounds equal $H_{\min}$ and $H_{\max}$ \cite[Sections~5.2--5.3]{boydvandenberghe2004}. With rational inputs, a reader can use exact arithmetic to check the feasibility of candidate cell totals and multipliers and verify that the weighted sum of those cell totals equals the corresponding dual bound.

Keep the partition and target fixed. Widening the observation limits replaces $b$ by $b+\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon\geq0$ and unchanged constraints have zero increments. The original multipliers enclose all feasible target values in
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:relaxed-certificates}
 [-\mu^\mathsf{T}(b+\varepsilon),\;\lambda^\mathsf{T}(b+\varepsilon)].
\end{equation}
The original multipliers remain feasible because the dual inequalities omit $b$ \cite[Section~5.6]{boydvandenberghe2004}. Optimising again gives the exact endpoints if the revised feasible set is nonempty. The measurement procedure must justify the wider limits.

Suppose sample-based limits cover all true observation totals simultaneously with probability at least $1-\alpha$, for $0<\alpha<1$. Whenever they do and the true history satisfies the capacity constraints, its cell totals belong to $\mathcal P$ and its target lies in the calculated interval. Thus, under those constraints, target coverage is at least $1-\alpha$, counting an empty feasible set as a failure.

Choose individual bounds whose failure probabilities sum to at most $\alpha$. The union bound then gives simultaneous coverage of at least $1-\alpha$ under arbitrary dependence.\footnote{If $E_\nu$ denotes failure of bound $\nu$, then $\Prb(\bigcup_{\nu=1}^M E_\nu)\leq\sum_{\nu=1}^M\Prb(E_\nu)$.} The sampling design and measurement procedure must justify these probabilities.

The Lean~4 proofs use mathlib \cite{demouraullrich2021,mathlib2020} to connect interval integrals to cell totals under each person's capacity constraint. For every feasible array they verify a schedule that gives those totals and their weighted targets with at most one activity per person at any instant. Section~\ref{sec:accounting} counts concurrent AI execution separately.

For supplied feasible allocations and multipliers, Lean checks the bounds. It also verifies that simultaneous observation coverage implies target coverage. The arguments above establish the existence of optimisers and matching multipliers. They also bound the number of blocks. The Rust solver has separate tests. A proof that it implements the Lean definitions remains open.

\subsection{From labour bounds to productivity}

Let $Q=\sum_{i\in\mathcal C}Q_i(\tau,\Delta)$ be a known nonnegative follow-up score. If $H_{\min}>0$ in \eqref{eq:cohort-productivity}, continuity and monotonicity of $Q/H$ on $[H_{\min},H_{\max}]$ give the rate range $[Q/H_{\max},Q/H_{\min}]$.

For variable output $0\leq Q_-\leq Q\leq Q_+$ and $H_{\min}>0$, every feasible rate lies in $[Q_-/H_{\max},Q_+/H_{\min}]$. This interval is exact if and only if every rate in it comes from at least one feasible output--labour pair. It suffices, for example, that all pairs in $[Q_-,Q_+]\times[H_{\min},H_{\max}]$ are feasible.\footnote{Feasibility specifies which pairs can occur. Given a distribution on pairs, a copula relates it to the marginal distributions under Sklar's theorem \cite[Theorem~2]{haugh2016copulas}. Independence is a property of that distribution.} The optimisation over labour holds each task's acceptance time and retained requirement fraction fixed in \eqref{eq:retention}.
