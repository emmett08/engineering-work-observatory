\section{Attainable labour ranges from interval observations}
\label{sec:sharp-identification}

An interval total leaves the timing of attention within it unresolved. We therefore seek all values of fixed-horizon labour compatible with the observations and the allocation constraint in \eqref{eq:labour}. The \emph{identified range} contains exactly those values: each is attained by at least one compatible history, and every compatible history gives a value in the range. This use of compatibility follows the general partial-identification perspective \cite[Section~1]{torgovitsky2019}. We prove the allocation results directly. They describe what the observations determine, conditional on the observation bounds and allocation constraints.

\subsection{Using the person's capacity}

Knowing which person supplied an interval total bounds how much can fit on either side of a cutoff. An aggregate across several people requires their combined capacity.

\begin{proposition}[Attainable contribution from one interval]
\label{prop:sharp-cutoff}
Let $\ell<u$, $c\in[\ell,u]$ and $v\in[0,u-\ell]$. Among measurable allocations $a:[\ell,u]\to[0,1]$ with $\int_\ell^u a(t)\,dt=v$, the attainable values of $x=\int_\ell^c a(t)\,dt$ are exactly
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sharp-cutoff}
 \left[\max\{0,v-(u-c)\},\;\min\{v,c-\ell\}\right].
\end{equation}
If $v<0$ or $v>u-\ell$, no such allocation exists.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Nonnegativity and capacity on the two pieces imply
\[
 0\leq x\leq c-\ell,\qquad 0\leq v-x\leq u-c.
\]
Their intersection is \eqref{eq:sharp-cutoff}. Conversely, for any $x$ in this interval, set $a=x/(c-\ell)$ before $c$ and $a=(v-x)/(u-c)$ after $c$. Each value lies in $[0,1]$ and gives the required integral. Omit a zero-length piece when $c$ is an endpoint: the corresponding numerator is then zero. Finally, integrating $0\leq a\leq1$ gives the necessary condition $0\leq v\leq u-\ell$.
\end{proof}

For example, $3/2$ person-hours in a two-hour interval imply between $1/2$ and $1$ person-hours in its first hour. Nonnegativity alone would give $[0,3/2]$. The capacity bound still leaves the allocation within the interval unresolved. If $v=u-\ell$, capacity identifies every cutoff integral even though individual activity labels have not been supplied. For an interior subinterval $[b,c]\subseteq[\ell,u]$, the same construction on that subinterval and its complement gives the attainable marginal range $[\max\{0,v-((u-\ell)-(c-b))\},\min\{v,c-b\}]$.

Summing marginal ranges gives valid bounds for a nonduplicated partition of contributions. The summed lower and upper bounds are attainable only if the respective component allocations can coexist. Disjoint intervals of one person, or intervals belonging to distinct people, can be combined when no other constraints link them. Overlapping observations or several tasks competing for the same person's time require a joint calculation. For example, two task masses of one person-hour each on $[0,2]$ individually allow $[0,1]$ before time 1. Their sum is exactly one person-hour before time 1: the shared capacity is saturated throughout $[0,2]$. Independently adding the marginal intervals would give only $[0,2]$.

\subsection{An exact finite representation of compatible histories}

To express those shared constraints, call each task--person--role combination a stream. Fix finite sets of tasks $\mathcal I$, people $\mathcal J$ and roles $\mathcal R$, a cohort $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal I$, and a bounded calendar-time domain $[b_0,b_K]$ with $b_0<b_K$ containing every observation interval and target window. An admissible history consists of measurable $a_{ijr}\geq0$ satisfying $\sum_{i,r}a_{ijr}(t)\leq1$ almost everywhere for every person $j$. The declared roster and attribution rules define the labour being bounded, so evidence of their completeness is a measurement requirement. A residual category outside $\mathcal C$ can represent work that consumes capacity without contributing to the cohort target.

For observations $\nu=1,\ldots,M$, specify an interval $[\ell_\nu,u_\nu]$ within this domain, a set $G_\nu\subseteq\mathcal I\times\mathcal J\times\mathcal R$ of attributed streams, and finite labour bounds $v_\nu^-\leq v_\nu^+$, in person-hours. Impose
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:interval-information}
 v_\nu^-\leq
 \sum_{(i,j,r)\in G_\nu}\int_{\ell_\nu}^{u_\nu}a_{ijr}(t)\,dt
 \leq v_\nu^+.
\end{equation}
Exact interval totals use $v_\nu^-=v_\nu^+$. Deterministic rounding or ascertainment-error limits may instead justify a nonzero width. A sampled Horvitz--Thompson estimate supplies an estimated total, whose sampling uncertainty requires a separate analysis before either inequality can be imposed.

Form the sorted distinct endpoints $b_0<\cdots<b_K$ from the domain boundaries, every observation endpoint and every target boundary. For the cohort denominator, target boundaries include each cohort task's enrolment $s_i$ and its fixed cutoff $c_i=s_i+\tau+\Delta$. Translate task ages into calendar time before partitioning, so that simultaneous work by the same person shares one capacity constraint. Retain cells outside a target window whenever an observation spans them. Put $d_k=b_k-b_{k-1}>0$ and let $x_{ijrk}$ denote the mass of stream $(i,j,r)$ in cell $k$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact cell reduction and attainable labour range]
\label{prop:cell-reduction}
Under the preceding model, define the finite-dimensional feasible set $\mathcal P$ by
\begin{align}
\label{eq:cell-capacity}
 x_{ijrk}&\geq0,&\sum_{i,r}x_{ijrk}&\leq d_k
 &&\text{for every }j,k,\\
\label{eq:cell-information}
 v_\nu^-&\leq
 \sum_{(i,j,r)\in G_\nu}
 \sum_{k:\,[b_{k-1},b_k]\subseteq[\ell_\nu,u_\nu]}x_{ijrk}
 \leq v_\nu^+ &&\text{for every }\nu.
\end{align}
Fix real weights $w_{ijrk}$ and define the continuous target by weighting each cell integral by $w_{ijrk}$. For its finite representation $h(x)=\sum_{i,j,r,k}w_{ijrk}x_{ijrk}$, the attainable target values over admissible histories equal $h(\mathcal P)$. In particular, the fixed-horizon cohort denominator uses $w_{ijrk}=1$ exactly when $i\in\mathcal C$ and $[b_{k-1},b_k]\subseteq[s_i,c_i]$, and zero otherwise. If $\mathcal P$ is nonempty, its target range is the closed interval
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sharp-labour-range}
 [H_{\min},H_{\max}]
 =\left[\min_{x\in\mathcal P}h(x),\;\max_{x\in\mathcal P}h(x)\right].
\end{equation}
Both endpoints and every value between them are attained. If $\mathcal P$ is empty, the observations and capacity assumptions are inconsistent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Integrating any admissible history over each cell gives nonnegative masses. Integrating the shared capacity gives \eqref{eq:cell-capacity}. Splitting each observation at the cell boundaries gives \eqref{eq:cell-information}. The target is preserved because its weights are constant on cells.

Conversely, for $x\in\mathcal P$ set $a_{ijr}(t)=x_{ijrk}/d_k$ in the interior of cell $k$, and set the finitely many endpoint values to zero. These measurable allocations are nonnegative and have sum at most one for each person. They reproduce every cell mass, observation and target value. Thus the histories and the polytope have exactly the same target image.

The constraints define a closed convex set. Each coordinate is between zero and $d_k$, so a nonempty $\mathcal P$ is compact and a linear functional attains its extrema. Convex combinations of minimising and maximising points attain every intermediate value. The reconstruction just given supplies an admissible history for each of them.
\end{proof}

The identified range therefore requires two linear programmes, each with $|\mathcal I||\mathcal J||\mathcal R|K$ variables, $|\mathcal J|K$ shared-capacity constraints and $2M$ observation inequalities, in addition to nonnegativity. Replacing the continuous history by cell totals preserves every attainable target value. The constant allocations used in the proof reconstruct compatible histories exactly. A known period of unavailability can be included by adding its boundaries to the partition and setting that person's cell masses to zero within it. The reduction applies to the stated measurable allocations. Minimum session lengths, nonpreemption, smoothness and switching costs would impose additional restrictions requiring a separate analysis.

For the cohort-denominator target, let $Q=\sum_{i\in\mathcal C}Q_i(\tau,\Delta)$ be exact retained output. With $Q\geq0$ and $H_{\min}>0$, monotonicity and attainability give the identified productivity interval $[Q/H_{\max},Q/H_{\min}]$. Justified quality bounds $0\leq Q_-\leq Q\leq Q_+$ give an outer interval $[Q_-/H_{\max},Q_+/H_{\min}]$ containing every feasible ratio. It is the exact range only when every ratio in it is attainable from an admissible output--labour pair. Admissibility of the whole product $[Q_-,Q_+]\times[H_{\min},H_{\max}]$ is sufficient. A zero lower labour bound does not justify dividing by it. The acceptance clock and retention assessment in \eqref{eq:retention} remain fixed throughout the allocation optimisation.
