\subsection{Bounding labour at a bin boundary}
\label{sec:binning-bounds}

Exact labour totals for time bins can bound labour before a chosen task age even when the timing of work within each bin is unknown. Suppose the labour for a fixed set of tasks is fully covered by task-age bins $[\ell_j,u_j)$, with $0\leq\ell_j<u_j$, and each contribution, including shared work, is counted once. Each bin's labour is a nonnegative allocation integral with absolutely continuous cumulative labour and an exact total $v_j$ in person-hours, called its bin mass. Applying the bound requires independent checks that the bins cover all included labour and count each contribution once. Express task ages and cutoffs in the same time unit, preserving the exact bin totals when converting units. Rounded quantities require separate bounds on rounding error.

\begin{proposition}[A continuous labour envelope]
\label{prop:binning-bounds}
Under these premises, set $c=\tau+\Delta$ and $H(c)=\sum_i H_i(c)$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:binning-envelope}
 H_-(c):=\sum_jv_j\ind\{u_j\leq c\}
 \;\leq H(c)\leq\;
 H_+(c):=\sum_jv_j\ind\{\ell_j<c\}.
\end{equation}
The gap is $H_+(c)-H_-(c)=\sum_{j:\,\ell_j<c<u_j}v_j$. If $H_-(c)>0$ and the total score $Q=\sum_iQ_i(\tau,\Delta)$ is known, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:binning-ratio}
 \frac{Q}{H_+(c)}\leq P_H(\tau,\Delta)\leq\frac{Q}{H_-(c)}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A bin ending by $c$ contributes its full mass. A bin starting at or after $c$ contributes zero. Absolute continuity excludes endpoint atoms. Every crossing bin contributes between zero and its mass. Summing gives \eqref{eq:binning-envelope} and the gap. Nonnegative $Q$ and positive bounding denominators give \eqref{eq:binning-ratio} by division.
\end{proof}

When $H_-=0$, these bounds leave positivity of the labour denominator unresolved, so a finite upper productivity bound requires additional information. If the total score instead has justified bounds $Q_-\leq Q\leq Q_+$, positive $H_-$ gives $Q_-/H_+\leq P_H\leq Q_+/H_-$. These calculations require exact bin totals. A Horvitz--Thompson estimate varies across samples, so bounds based on its values require an analysis of sampling error and missing responses. Confidence intervals for an intervention's causal effect additionally require a study that compares interventions.

\begin{example}[An interior cutoff]
\label{ex:binning-cutoff}
One person's bin $[0,2)$ contains one person-hour. At $c=1$, work entirely in the first hour gives labour 1, whereas work entirely in the second gives 0. Both respect the allocation constraint. Uniform interpolation gives $1/2$, but the known bin mass alone supports the interval $[0,1]$.
\end{example}
