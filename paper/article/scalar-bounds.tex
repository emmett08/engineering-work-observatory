\section{A shortest-path solution for one allocation stream}
\label{app:scalar}

For one person performing one activity on a single task, let $0\leq a\leq1$ be the allocation function. At each calendar boundary in Section~\ref{sec:sharp-identification}, cumulative labour is $z_k=\int_{b_0}^{b_k}a(t)\,dt$. Represent each upper bound $z_q-z_p\leq w$ by a directed edge $p\to q$ of weight $w$ \cite[Slides~6--11]{indyk2011}.

Cell $k$ permits between zero and $d_k$ person-hours. Its forward edge $k-1\to k$ has weight $d_k$ and its reverse edge has weight zero. An observation bounding labour between $b_p$ and $b_q$ by $[v^-,v^+]$ adds edges $p\to q$ of weight $v^+$ and $q\to p$ of weight $-v^-$. For an exact total $v$, these weights are $v$ and $-v$.

Summing edge inequalities along a path cancels the cumulative labour terms at its intermediate vertices. Each path from $p$ to $q$ thus bounds $z_q-z_p$ from above by its weight. Paths in the reverse direction bound $z_p-z_q$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact range of interval labour from graph distances]
\label{prop:graph-bounds}
An allocation satisfying all the observations exists if and only if the graph has no directed cycle of negative total weight. In that case, let $D(p,q)$ denote the shortest-path weight from $p$ to $q$. For any endpoint pair $p\leq q$, the identified range of $\int_{b_p}^{b_q}a(t)\,dt$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:graph-range}
 [-D(q,p),\;D(p,q)].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Around a cycle the cumulative differences sum to zero, so any feasible set of totals requires nonnegative cycle weights. Conversely, suppose there is no negative cycle. Edges between adjacent vertices give paths in both directions, so all shortest-path weights are finite and $D(r,r)=0$ for each starting vertex $r$. For an edge $u\to v$ of weight $w$, the path inequality $D(r,v)\leq D(r,u)+w$ shows that $z_k=D(r,k)-D(r,0)$ satisfies the edge constraint and $z_0=0$. The adjacent edges imply $0\leq z_k-z_{k-1}\leq d_k$. Working at the fraction $(z_k-z_{k-1})/d_k$ throughout cell $k$ gives exactly its prescribed hours.

The shortest paths in the two directions give $-D(q,p)\leq z_q-z_p\leq D(p,q)$. Taking $r=p$ and $r=q$ gives schedules at the upper and lower endpoints, respectively. Their convex combinations satisfy all constraints and give every intermediate total.
\end{proof}

The reference implementation uses exact rational arithmetic. For $K+1$ sorted, indexed boundaries and $M$ observations, building the graph and computing all pairwise distances takes $O((K+1)^3+M)$ arithmetic operations and $O((K+1)^2+M)$ storage. The bit cost per operation depends on operand size. In finite examples with overlapping intervals, an independent enumeration checks the endpoints, their schedules and detection of inconsistent observations.

Separate graphs for tasks sharing one person's time can count the same hour twice. Proposition~\ref{prop:cell-reduction} imposes a single time limit across that person's activities. For both methods, specify the contributors and activities covered by each observation.
