\textbf{Shared capacity determines the combined first-hour labour.}
In this constructed example, one person spends one person-hour on each of two tasks during two hours.
In (a), $x$ and $y$ are the tasks' first-hour labour totals.
Every point in the shaded square satisfies the separate bounds. Enforcing the person's capacity in both hours restricts the feasible points to the diagonal.
In (b), adding the separate bounds permits between zero and two person-hours, whereas the joint constraints require exactly one.
In (c), one full-attention schedule attains the marked point $P=(0.5,0.5)$ by giving each task half an hour in each hour, with one activity at a time.
The example allows the person to switch tasks at arbitrary times. The schedule is neither unique nor observed.
