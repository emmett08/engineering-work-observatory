\textbf{Shared capacity determines the combined first-hour labour.}
In this numerical example, one person spends one person-hour on each of two tasks during two hours.
In (a), $x$ and $y$ are the tasks' first-hour labour totals.
Each point in the shaded square satisfies the bounds calculated for the tasks separately. Applying the person's capacity limit in both hours leaves only the diagonal as feasible.
In (b), adding the separate bounds permits between zero and two person-hours, whereas the joint constraints require exactly one.
In (c), one schedule with a single activity at a time achieves the marked point $P=(0.5,0.5)$ by giving each task half an hour in each hour.
The example allows the person to switch tasks at arbitrary times. The schedule illustrates one of the possible arrangements under these assumptions.
