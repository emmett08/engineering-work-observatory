\textbf{Shared capacity determines total labour in the first hour.}
In this numerical example, one person spends one person-hour on each of two tasks during two hours.
In (a), $x$ and $y$ give the labour on each task during the first hour.
Each point in the shaded square satisfies the bounds calculated for the tasks separately. Accounting for the person's available time in both hours leaves only the diagonal as possible.
In (b), adding the separate bounds permits a combined first-hour total between zero and two person-hours. Accounting jointly for both tasks requires exactly one.
In (c), one schedule with a single activity at a time achieves the marked point $P=(0.5,0.5)$ by giving each task half an hour in each hour.
The example allows the person to switch tasks at arbitrary times. The schedule illustrates one of the possible arrangements under these assumptions.
