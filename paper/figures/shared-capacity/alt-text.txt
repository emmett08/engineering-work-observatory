Hypothetical example of one person spending one person-hour on each of two tasks during two hours. Panel (a) has first-hour task A labour on the horizontal axis and first-hour task B labour on the vertical axis, both from zero to one person-hour. A light square contains all pairs permitted by the separate bounds. A descending dark line links zero person-hours for A and one for B to one person-hour for A and zero for B. Points on that line satisfy the capacity limits in both hours. Points above it exceed the first-hour limit. Points below it would require excess work in the second hour. A diamond marks P, where both first-hour task totals equal half a person-hour. Panel (b) shows that adding separate bounds permits a combined first-hour total from zero to two person-hours, while joint constraints permit only one. Panel (c) gives one possible schedule for P. Task A receives the person's full attention from 0 to 0.5 hours and B from 0.5 to 1. The person then works on A from 1 to 1.5 and B from 1.5 to 2. A dashed divider marks the first-hour boundary. Every interval contains one activity at a time. The timeline gives one schedule that achieves these totals under the assumption that tasks may be interrupted at arbitrary times. Other schedules can achieve the same totals, and the example describes a hypothetical allocation rather than observed work. No panel encodes probability or a causal effect.
