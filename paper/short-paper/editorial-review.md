# Editorial review

The review covers the short manuscript, including headings, captions and table prose. It also covers the model notes and the prose in the argument files. The counts below apply to the short manuscript. They exclude equations, identifiers, citation markers, URLs and bibliography entries. The count uses case-insensitive word forms and excludes common function words. It counts inflections separately.

## Negative Syndeton

No grouped denials remain. The workplace section now describes a shared omission directly by asking the reader to allow for hours both sources could omit.

## Contrastive Framing

1. **[Introduction](main.tex)**. “The corresponding expected active times over an hour are 30 and 15 minutes.” Keep this comparison. The two models predict the same observable commits. Their differing expected times establish why the observations cannot distinguish the two activity rates.
2. **[Results](results.tex)**. “Omitting the reviewer left the mean width at 0.5 person-hours and excluded the actual total in 26 cases.” Keep the unchanged width beside the newly excluded totals. Both facts are needed to explain why width alone cannot establish coverage.

## Tetracolon

None found. The numerical comparisons use a table with defined columns.

## Grammatical Parallelism

3. **[Paired trial](workplace.tex)**. “Declare a benefit only when the upper limit for the labour difference falls below $-1$. The lower limit for the retained fraction difference must also exceed $-0.05$.” Each condition now has its own sentence. Both must hold for a benefit claim.
4. **[Sampling](sampling.tex)**. The paragraph on unanswered replies now introduces monotonicity before evaluating the weighted estimate at the two endpoints. That sequence explains why the endpoint substitutions bound the complete-measurement estimate.

## Syndeton

5. **[Time constraints](bounds.tex)**. “The feasible set is closed and bounded, so both extrema exist.” The two properties justify compactness in the finite-dimensional model. Removing either property would weaken that proof.
6. **[Results](results.tex)**. The setup now describes the repair placements and review placements in successive sentences before multiplying their counts. The reader can see where the 96 schedules come from.

## Over-elaboration

7. **[Opening example](main.tex)**. The worked example now ends with **evidence that the engineer reviewed the change throughout the second half**. This supplies the observation that fixes the first-half total at one hour. The two sentences that repeated the shortcut were removed.
8. **[Time partition](bounds.tex)**. State why an observation may bring out-of-target work into the model before introducing the time cells. The revised paragraph places the **full span of each observation** beside the shared observed total it constrains.
9. **[Workplace checks](workplace.tex)**. “To check for omitted hours, select shifts independently of whether contributors replied.” The reason for selecting shifts comes first. The paragraph then explains how to establish attendance before addressing the follow-up score.

10. **[Trial planning](workplace.tex)**. “Set the rules for bounding unobserved outcomes before the trial begins.” The next paragraph defines the range covering both possible allocations before it explains how to bound the difference for the allocation the pair received.
11. **[Estimating the trial effect](workplace.tex)**. The effect is defined before the radius is introduced. The passage uses the mean from the allocation draw to connect the measurement intervals to the confidence limits.
