# How Much Human Work Is Behind AI-Assisted Software?

Review of the manuscript at [804f073](https://github.com/emmett08/engineering-work-observatory/tree/804f07357511794493b5a9bd95675f60e0604f0e/paper/article) and the changes in this revision.

## 1. Executive critique

A person's available hours can rule out combinations of task totals that would each be possible in isolation. With a different source of evidence, a probability sample covering all eligible person-intervals, a team can correct systematic errors in its labour predictions. Both results depend on knowing whose work the measurements cover. Off-keyboard work, unanswered prompts during incidents and contributions from external reviewers can all undermine a field application while its arithmetic remains correct.

The proposed trial faces a separate mathematical limit. Its benefit rule requires complete endpoints from every team. In the simulation scenario where each independent pair has a 0.5% chance of an unresolved endpoint, even perfect measurements on complete pairs would leave the chance of declaring benefit below 90% at either proposed size. The measurement pilot is the appropriate next study. The paper reports no workplace estimate of an AI productivity effect. ## 3. Mathematical review and formal refinements

**Empirical reference for forecasting person-hours.** For a task of class $x$ arriving at time $c$, define $D_c(x)$ using only earlier tasks whose full labour window and independently measured hours were available by $c$. The proposed benchmark is $g_c(x)=\sum_{j\in D_c(x)}H_j(u)/|D_c(x)|$ at the same task age $u$ for every task. Verified zero hours enter both the sum and the count. Missing hours require an assumption or a narrower target population. Define a minimum history and a broader comparable class before evaluation. If neither class contains enough observations, count the task among those for which the method could issue no forecast. After a change of staffing or tools, inspect signed errors by period and compare predictions on the same subsequent intakes. Report the fraction of eligible tasks for which each method issued a prediction. A historical class mean is a benchmark awaiting prospective evaluation.

**Horvitz–Thompson and the difference estimator.** Let $y_m$ be actual person-hours in interval $m$, $f_m$ its prior prediction and $\pi_m>0$ its known selection probability. The estimator $\sum_U f_m+\sum_{m\in s}(y_m-f_m)/\pi_m$ is design-unbiased for total hours when the draws leave the predictions fixed and sampled hours are measured accurately. Taking $f_m=0$ gives Horvitz–Thompson. Under simple random sampling without replacement with $n<M$, the prediction improves precision exactly when residual hours vary less across the frame than the hours themselves. Sampling one interval per shift makes it impossible to observe two different intervals from that shift in the same draw. The usual observed-pairs variance estimate then lacks the required within-shift information. [Estimator, proof and variance equations](../article/design-based-estimation.tex) · [Aronow and Samii](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-001-x/2013001/article/11831-eng.htm)

**Missing responses.** Randomly follow up some missed prompts with conditional probability $\eta_m>0$ and recover the original interval's labour. Weight the recovered residual by $1/(\pi_m\eta_m)$ and initial replies by $1/\pi_m$. Conditioning first on the sample and initial replies shows why the second draw recovers their missing residual total in expectation even if the busiest people seldom answer the initial prompt. This extension still needs complete, accurate responses among the selected follow-ups and uncertainty from both draws. The current software rejects incomplete sampled allocations. Persistently missing hours need sensitivity analysis. [Follow-up sampling](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-001-x/2022001/article/00006-eng.htm)

**Attainable capacity bounds.** If one person worked $v$ hours across intervals of lengths $a,b$, with $0\le v\le a+b$, the first contains between $\max(0,v-b)$ and $\min(v,a)$ hours. Allocating $h$ hours to the first and $v-h$ to the second constructs every feasible value. The general proof uses cells split at every observation and target boundary and allows arbitrary interruption within cells. Suppose two engineers each spent an hour on the same review in a two-hour period, and one was available only in the first hour. Capacity permits one to two person-hours in that hour. Confirmation that the review took place together fixes the total at two. The paper now shows which extra evidence narrows the feasible set. If several input limits are estimated, the final bounds inherit a coverage guarantee from the probability that all the input limits hold simultaneously. [Theorem and proof](../article/partial-identification.tex) · [Field interpretation](../article/interpreting-capacity.tex)

**Productivity and uncertain totals.** Compute $P=Q/H$ from the sums of retained reference scores and attributable hours for the same intake cohort, with $H>0$. An automated task can contribute output with zero labour. A task that misses the deadline can contribute labour with zero scored output. If known $Q>0$ and attainable $H$ spans $[0,U]$ for $U>0$, positive-denominator rates span $[Q/U,\infty)$. The zero-hour history has an undefined rate. If the same evidence constrains $Q$ and $H$, optimise over feasible pairs. With $Q=2H$ for $1\le H\le2$, the true rate is exactly 2 while separate marginal bounds admit 1 through 4.

Sampling requires a second argument. With six output units and interval labour of one and three hours, sampling one interval uniformly gives an unbiased total-hours estimate of 2 or 6. The mean of the estimated rates is 2, while the actual rate is 1.5. To give a confidence set, invert a valid interval for $Q-pH$ under the sampling procedure used for both totals. Its variance includes the covariance of the two estimated totals. Report any unbounded set together with both component estimates. [Ratio inference](../article/design-based-estimation.tex) · [von Luxburg and Franz](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.0198)

## 4. Real-world deployment roadmap

| Field question | Pilot procedure | Evidence for the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whose work is missing? | List contributors from task intake and duty schedules, including reviewers, incident responders and after-hours cover. Check their eligible working periods against that list. Keep a known hour of cohort work even if its precise ticket is unknown, provided every plausible task belongs to the cohort and the work falls inside each task’s labour window. | Report covered roles and hours, unknown attribution and plausible changes in the total across the tasks to which the work could belong. |
| Can people sustain the measurement? | Compare the planned 15-minute ledger with four randomly sampled intervals on comparable person-days. Randomise within contributor and shift type. Independently select audit days in each group. | Compare minutes spent reporting, nonresponse and discrepancies in person-hours. Before looking at the differences, set the largest error and reporting burden the decision could tolerate. |
| Does packaging change the apparent output? | Give independent reviewers the same requirement delivered through one PR and three PRs, keeping behaviour, dates and follow-up identical. Then move one component past the deadline. | The retained score should agree in the first comparison. The second should reflect the declared rule for counting a parent or its accepted components. Report reviewer disagreement and severe defects separately. |
| Can the trial ever reach its decision? | Under independent pair loss $q$, the all-complete rule caps success at $(1-q)^m$ for $m$ pairs. At $q=0.005$, the ceilings are 88.7% and 78.6% for 24 and 48 pairs. Even zero losses in six pilot pairs gives a one-sided 95% upper bound of 39.3% on $q$. | Use the pilot to diagnose loss mechanisms. Before a full trial, obtain enough independent evidence to justify a loss rate compatible with the 90% target, or specify a bounded-missing-outcome decision and evaluate its error rates and power. |

The proposed experiment compares teams on labour and retained output per **initial** reference unit, with equal weight per team. These are the two primary outcomes. Pooling the teams’ scores and hours gives the observed reference units delivered per person-hour across the whole cohort.
